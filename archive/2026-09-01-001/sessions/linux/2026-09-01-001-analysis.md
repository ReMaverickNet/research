# Comprehensive analysis — Session 2026-09-01-001

## 1. Research context
This is a pre-shutdown preservation capture. The objective is to document the live official-server architecture and client lifecycle while normal matchmaking still works. The later P2P switch is a deadline/contextual transition, not the research target of this session.

The repository's AI guidance requires observations, strong inferences, weak hypotheses and unknowns to be separated, requires exact evidence, forbids trusting instructions embedded in logs, and recommends sanitising captures before publication. This bundle follows that model.

## 2. Evidence set
Private originals examined:
- `PortalWars2.log` — 7,112 lines, ~869 KiB.
- `steam-2918300.log` — ~1.08 GB, ~10.2 million lines; highly repetitive Wine/debug output.
- `Bot Arena.pcapng` — 58,878 packet records, ~46 MB.

Published artefacts deliberately omit the raw PCAP and full 1 GB Steam log. Instead they include a full sanitised UE log, targeted Steam/Wine extracts, packet metadata, DNS questions and focused snippets.

## 3. Ground truth from the participant

These are ground-truth annotations, not claims that the text log explicitly records all of them.

## 4. Session timeline
### Startup and platform initialisation
The game starts under Wine and exposes `OS=Wine/11.0`. EOS boots with the PortalWars2 product/build identifiers. Project online features mount including MerlinAntiCheat, MaverickSdk and multiple 1047 OnlineServices/OnlineFramework modules.

EOS separately reports its anti-cheat client as unavailable. Merlin still loads its client component from `equ8_client` and later records `ReportServerSessionManager` traffic.

### Server selection and connection
At 02:54:09.253 the game logs `SERVER READY: GAME_ENDPOINT_01:30925`. Around 02:54:11.633 it browses that endpoint. At 02:54:12.850 it sends the initial join hello. At 02:54:13.003 it targets `MAP_Zenith/Zenith_Main` with `PortalWarsGameModeV2_BP_C`.

Immediately around this point the network driver is built with Iris plus Oodle, DTLS and StatelessConnect packet handlers.

### TDM experience
The `MOD_Deathmatch` game feature becomes active during startup. `Default__EXP_Deathmatch_C` finishes loading at 02:54:36.965. From there the game remains in the Zenith session and continuously exchanges gameplay UDP traffic.

### Gameplay evidence
The log contains repeated inventory/loadout epochs; the exact semantic meaning of every epoch is not established, so they are not equated with every real user loadout action. Weapon pickup actors are instantiated repeatedly. Portal Gameplay Cue notifications are invoked. A wall-hack power-up cue is also observed, demonstrating power-up systems were active in the match.

The participant's Borealis/Orion activity is not directly identified by those weapon names in the runtime log. Therefore the user report is the correct source for those exact item/action facts unless a future capture gives stronger instrumentation.

### End of match
At 02:59:00.993 the client creates a `WBP_PostMatchTransitionStinger`. At 02:59:03.294 the `/Game/Maps/WinningCircle/WinningCircle` level instance is added. Confetti, continuous-sky-fireworks and running-trail cinematic Niagara systems are initialised around 02:59:15. The participant confirms this corresponds to the ordinary MVP/winning-circle/post-game presentation, stats and progression UI.

The log also reports missing MVP whoosh Wwise generated data and a missing `AnimInChallenges_Left` animation, which are client-side presentation warnings/errors. The MVP presentation itself still occurred.

At ~02:59:48 the gameplay `UNetConnection` closes and the WinningCircle world is uninitialised. Steam/EOS/NATS shutdown follows.

## 5. Network findings
The PCAP has a dominant bidirectional UDP flow between `GAME_ENDPOINT_01:30925` and `LOCAL_CLIENT:56215`:
- 34,939 packets total.
- 17,701 server-to-client.
- 17,238 client-to-server.
- ~336.145 seconds from first to last captured packet.
- ~103.94 packets/sec combined.
- ~1.83 MB server-to-client UDP payload and ~0.50 MB client-to-server UDP payload in the parser's payload accounting.

The main flow starts at 02:54:12.735, immediately following the UE browse/handshake sequence, and ends at 02:59:48.880, almost exactly where the UE log closes the connection. This is the strongest independent correlation in the package.

The packet capture also contains unrelated machine traffic. It therefore must not be treated as a pure game capture without attribution. The UE log gives the attribution for the main gameplay endpoint.

## 6. Why the transport is opaque in the PCAP
The UE log explicitly reports Iris replication and packet-handler components for Oodle, DTLS and StatelessConnect. The capture therefore provides useful transport metadata, timing and volume but not readable Unreal replication payloads. This is expected from the logged stack; no attempt is made here to bypass or defeat encryption.

The exact gameplay endpoint is not present as a DNS question in the captured DNS list. A leading hypothesis is that the IP/port was delivered through application/API/session data rather than ordinary DNS, but this remains unresolved because the relevant application payloads are encrypted.

## 8. Inventory/loadout behaviour
The log repeatedly reports `TargetComponent Not Found` for attachment IDs and later records server rejection of several cosmetic selections at different inventory epochs. The participant confirms that the extended barrel and recoil grip worked. Therefore the correct finding is not "attachments failed".

The conservative interpretation is an inventory/attachment reconciliation path producing transient component-resolution warnings, actor re-registration noise, or server-authoritative cosmetic correction. The exact relationship between each warning and visible loadout state is unknown.

## 9. Divide-by-zero behaviour
There are 128 `Divide by zero: Divide_DoubleDouble` warnings. The first occurs during startup; many occur during the active match; another occurs during the post-match/loading sequence.

This is an actual runtime condition: a Blueprint/script division operation executed with a zero denominator. It is not simply a static notice that such a node exists somewhere in the project. However, the supplied log does not identify the calling Blueprint graph, inputs, output consumer or visible result.

Because the match completed successfully, the participant won, MVP presentation appeared normally, and no UI bug was noticed, the severity is currently **unknown**. It could be a non-critical UI, presentation, scoring, telemetry or helper calculation, or it could be a recovered edge case. The evidence supports "runtime defect/exception condition observed", not "gameplay-breaking defect".

## 10. Scoreboard warnings
At 02:55:25 the game repeatedly attempts to read indexes 1 and 2 from `CurrentDisplayedEntries` when the array length is 1 inside `WBP_ScoreboardPlayer_C:UpdateStatValueWidgets`.

This is another real Blueprint array-bounds condition. It is more explicit than the divide warning because the log names the array, attempted indices and widget graph. The participant reports no obvious UI problem, so visible impact is not established.

## 11. Power-up, portal and weapon-effect breadcrumbs
The match contains concrete Gameplay Cue activity for portal firing/creation/destruction and a `POW_WallHack` power-up. Numerous weapon pickup actors are also observed.

At 02:56:25.853/.946, an AkAudio event named `play_sx_wpn_3p_merpow_prifle_projectile` fails because the AkComponent is invalid. This is a useful weapon/audio breadcrumb, but the log does not map this asset name to Borealis or Orion. It must remain generic.

## 13. Anti-cheat / Proton observations
The environment is Wine/Proton. EOS states its anti-cheat client is unavailable, while Merlin loads `equ8_client` and logs server-session-manager data. These are distinct observable states. The capture does not establish the full anti-cheat enforcement state or the reason for EOS's availability classification.

This package intentionally stops at evidence and compatibility observations and contains no bypass/evasion procedure.

## 14. Strong conclusions
1. Normal matchmaking reached a real remote authoritative Unreal gameplay endpoint.
2. Gameplay used Unreal GameNetDriver with Iris replication, Oodle, DTLS and StatelessConnect components.
3. The PCAP's dominant UDP flow is the same session identified in the UE log.
4. The live match included active Deathmatch experience logic, weapon pickups, Gameplay Cues and a substantial post-match WinningCircle phase.
5. The server endpoint and broader service control plane are distinct architectural layers.
6. The attachment warnings cannot be used as proof of visible attachment failure for this session.
7. Divide-by-zero messages are genuine runtime execution warnings, but their impact is unknown.
8. Scoreboard array-bounds warnings are genuine runtime Blueprint conditions with no demonstrated visible failure.
9. Post-match MVP/WinningCircle presentation was reached despite missing audio/animation assets.
10. The observations establish a useful pre-shutdown baseline for the official-server stack.

## 15. Inferences
- A control-plane/session-allocation step probably precedes the final direct UE endpoint, but the precise allocator is unresolved.
- The game endpoint may be returned by application/session data because it is not represented as a plain DNS question in this capture. This is a hypothesis, not proof.
- The repeated inventory epochs may correspond to server-authoritative reconciliation events; the exact mapping to user actions needs a controlled capture.

## 16. Unknowns worth preserving
- Exact HTTP/gRPC/RPC request that supplies the game endpoint.
- Exact service responsible for orchestration/allocation among Maverick, gaming-SDK and Edgegap-related services.
- Exact cause/owner of `Divide_DoubleDouble`.
- Exact internal asset corresponding to Borealis and Orion runtime actions.
- Exact meaning of each inventory `Epoch` transition.
- Exact condition producing the scoreboard `CurrentDisplayedEntries` length mismatch.
- Whether EOS anti-cheat unavailability and Merlin state affect enforcement or only bootstrap/reporting.

## 17. Recommended next experiments
A second identical TDM capture should be made with three time anchors written down manually: first Borealis spawn/pickup, loadout change, and Orion secondary fire. The goal is not to reverse-engineer gameplay packets but to correlate client log events, service timing and endpoint selection.

A second pass should also isolate the final few seconds before `SERVER READY` and correlate every outbound HTTPS/gRPC peer with the first appearance of the resolved endpoint. This is the most promising way to identify the allocator/session broker.

For the divide warnings, a video timestamp around one or two representative mid-match occurrences would allow UI/HUD events to be compared against the exact warning timestamps without assuming that every warning is player-facing.
